# DX-014: Serverless cost candidate

[Catalog](../detectors.md) | [Access and sign-in](../permissions.md)

## Data and scope

Serverless is an **account** capacity model, not an individual container setting. CODA
first checks the ARM account configuration and throughput inventory. An already-serverless
account, a second region (including a read replica), a
[shared-throughput database](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/cosmos-db/serverless#use-serverless-resources),
[free tier](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/cosmos-db/free-tier), enabled
[burst capacity](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/cosmos-db/burst-capacity#requirements), or enabled
[priority-based execution](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/cosmos-db/priority-based-execution#limitations)
makes this check **not applicable**. A single shared database rules out evaluating the account,
even when it also contains dedicated offers.

An unreadable or incomplete inventory, missing region/feature evidence, unclassified
capabilities, or an API outside the verified NoSQL offer inventory means **eligibility
unverified**. Neither outcome triggers DX-014 usage or pricing reads. CODA does not advise
removing a region or feature to obtain a hypothetical saving.

## Detection and severity

On a compatible account, CODA shows account-total `TotalRequestUnits` from the first
measurement, including today's observations. For economics it needs each of the 720
consecutive completed hourly totals across the **last 30 complete UTC billing dates**.
Today's open billing date cannot fill a gap. Missing/invalid observations are not zero.

| Configuration | Default |
|---|---|
| `required_hours` | `720` |

Following the [provisioned versus serverless comparison](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/cosmos-db/throughput-serverless#estimate-your-expected-consumption),
CODA compares actual account-attributed provisioned throughput charges (`100 RU/s`) with
a serverless estimate for those exact dates. For each day, it prices the day's measured
RU in millions at the **same subscription and region's** effective `1M RUs` price
(amortized cost divided by billed usage); then it sums the daily estimates. Prices are
from Cost Management, not a hardcoded list or an assumed exchange rate. Billing must cover
every day, in one currency, for both meters. The benchmark currently requires a verified
non-zone-redundant region; a zone-adjusted consumed-RU price is not inferred.

**Example (illustrative, not a quoted price):** suppose the account consumed 20 million RU,
its provisioned throughput charge was 30 currency units, and each day's observed regional
price was 0.25 units per million RU. The estimated serverless throughput cost would be
5 units. That earns **Low: evaluate serverless**, not “migrate.” At the same rate, 250 million
RU would cost 62.50 units and would not qualify, even if the two workloads had the same peak.
A tie is not a cost opportunity.

## Evidence and limits

Without a comparable month of billing (for example, no regional serverless meter in the
subscription, billing lag, denied access, or unmatched currency), CODA shows consumption
but issues no cost finding. A regional effective price is a benchmark, not a quote for a new
account; storage, networking, data movement, and future workload changes are not priced.
Average-to-peak ratio neither establishes nor rules out a cost opportunity. Hourly account totals
do not establish per-partition burst capacity or latency.

## Remediation and references

Review the measured cost comparison and application requirements. CODA does not prescribe migration,
attach a migration rule, or add this comparison to monthly savings.
