# DX-011: Autoscale maximum too high

[Catalog](../detectors.md) | [Access and sign-in](../permissions.md)

## Data and scope

Evaluates autoscale throughput offers, including shared databases. The signal is
`AutoscaledRU` summed across physical partitions at each timestamp, expressed as a percentage
of the configured maximum. `NormalizedRUConsumption` alone has a different autoscale denominator.

Production requests 30 days at hourly grain. Each hourly sum must include the number of
physical partitions independently measured throughout the hour by `PhysicalPartitionCount`
at its five-minute grain. A missing count or contribution is not zero. ARM supplies the
maximum; no Log Analytics is needed. A known creation date excludes earlier samples. `AutoscaledRU` has
no offer-owner dimension: CODA does not use a mixed database's aggregate as the shared pool's
provisioned level. Its dedicated siblings are evaluated separately.
Azure [documents `AutoscaledRU` for dynamic autoscale](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/cosmos-db/provision-throughput-autoscale#monitoring-metrics).
If no usable series is exposed, CODA cannot size the maximum from this signal.

## Detection and severity

Use the **maximum**, not p99, percentage of configured maximum. Idle is below 1%, low is
from 1% to below 10%, moderate is from 10% to below 30%, otherwise OK. Exactly 10% is moderate.
The shared peak-saturation guard also makes peaks at or above 90% OK.

| Configuration | Default |
|---|---|
| `band_statistic` | `"max"` |
| `idle_pct` | `1.0` |
| `low_pct` | `10.0` |
| `moderate_pct` | `30.0` |
| `AUTOSCALE_MIN_MAX_RU` | `1000` |
| `headroom` | `1.3` |
| `peak_saturation_pct` | `90.0` |

The target covers the observed peak with 30% headroom, never goes below the **1,000 RU/s
configurable maximum baseline**, and does not exceed the current maximum. At that baseline,
autoscale can scale down to **100 RU/s** when idle. The manual-throughput minimum of 400 RU/s
is a different constraint and is not used by this detector.

This remains an **analytical target**, not a service-ready setting. Accepted increments and
resource-specific minimum maxima can raise the actual target; storage, throughput history, and
shared-database constraints are not inferred by this detector. Verify service eligibility before
an update and reassess recovery using the eligible maximum.

Recoverable capacity is the reduction in the modeled billing floor:
`0.1 * current_max - 0.1 * recommended_max`, bounded below by zero.
It is **not** the full maximum reduction. Severity uses recoverable capacity: High at 5%
of scope capacity, Medium at 1%, otherwise Low, or absolute fallbacks of 5,000/1,000 RU/s.

## Evidence and limits

### When to suggest a lower autoscale maximum

Show actual scaled throughput relative to the configured maximum from the first measurement.
After **7 measured days** of low demand, call it out for review, without a lower ceiling,
recoverable RU/s, or estimated savings. A specific lower maximum requires **30 fully measured
days**, a qualifying observed peak, and the existing headroom and minimum-maximum safeguards.

Both windows end at the last completed UTC hour. For review, the **latest 168 consecutive
hours** must be fully measured for this offer; for a target, the **latest 720** must be.
Do not substitute an older quiet week or count the current open hour. If a physical
partition's contribution is missing, the partial sum is not the offer's measured throughput
for that hour. A gap yesterday means observations only. A gap 20 days ago can still leave
a complete recent week for review, but cannot justify a lower maximum or savings.

Example: with a 10,000 RU/s maximum and a 1,000 RU/s observed peak in week one, report those
two values, **not** “lower the maximum to 1,300.” If the full month still peaks at 1,000,
1,300 RU/s is an analytical target, subject to service eligibility. A peak of 9,500 at month-end
instead triggers the saturation safeguard. Any eligible savings reflect the reduction in the
autoscale floor, not the whole reduction in the maximum.

A 10,000 RU/s maximum with an observed 1,000 RU/s peak produces a 1,300 RU/s analytical target
and 870 recoverable RU/s (floors of 1,000 and 130). The 1,300 target is not rounded to a
service-accepted increment. A 4,000 maximum peaking at 400 produces a 1,000 target and
300 recoverable RU/s (floors of 400 and 100), not zero and not the full 3,000 maximum reduction.
With no scope denominator these examples are Low severity, despite their moderate utilization band.

At a 1,000 maximum with a 100 peak, the target remains 1,000 and recovery is zero. The unchanged
utilization-band policy can still report a Low finding; it does not imply a smaller eligible maximum.

Without any complete partition total the detector abstains; access/query failures are errors.
Resource age is not a substitute for measurements. Past provisioning does not guarantee future demand. Hourly billing reflects the highest
throughput actually scaled to in the hour, with a 10% minimum. The idle-floor estimate is not an
hour-by-hour bill simulation or guaranteed savings; any currency estimate must retain its separate
pricing provenance. CODA prices the floor delta in each configured region at the matching
meter rate: 1.5 times the quantity for single-write autoscale, with no extra factor on
the multi-write meter. It never prices the full maximum reduction or takes 10% twice.
An idle finding may supersede this finding.
