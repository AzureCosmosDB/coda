# DX-003: Shared-throughput starvation

[Catalog](../detectors.md) | [Log prerequisites](../detections/log-analytics-readiness.md)

## Data and scope

A database-level check selected for shared-throughput offers. It compares per-collection
request counts, 429 counts and consumed RU in **matching five-minute UTC intervals** within
a rolling one-day lookback. No minimum database age or full day of traffic is required. It prefers
`CDBDataPlaneRequests5M`, with raw `CDBDataPlaneRequests` compatibility when the aggregate
source is unavailable. Aggregate counts sum represented requests, not rows.
Five-minute windows end at a completed bucket; explicit windows must align with that source.
Only ARM-confirmed shared members contribute. A dedicated sibling is evaluated separately.
Unresolved membership or missing required member rows abstain this pool comparison, not the
whole account. A present zero-valued row is distinct from an absent row.

## Detection and severity

| Configuration | Default |
|---|---|
| `min_requests` | `100` |
| `pool_throttle_pct` | `5.0` |
| `dominance_pct` | `60.0` |
| `victim_throttle_pct` | `5.0` |
| `victim_share_pct` | `20.0` |

Apply these gates independently in each completed five-minute interval. Only collections
with at least 100 requests **in that interval** participate in its totals.
At least two must be active. Their combined 429 rate must be at least 5%, the largest
RU consumer must hold at least 60% of their RU, and a different collection must throttle
at least 5% while consuming at most 20%. All boundary comparisons are inclusive. These are shares of
observed RU consumption by active members, not shares of the configured RU/s.

| What the records establish | What CODA reports |
|---|---|
| Requests were throttled, but the high RU use and the sibling's 429s happened at different times | Show measured 429s. No contention finding and no attribution to that workload. |
| The pair meets all gates in one completed interval | **Possible contention**, an observation without severity or a migration recommendation. |
| The **same** dominant and affected containers meet every gate in **two consecutive completed five-minute intervals** | **High: likely shared-pool contention.** Show the names, interval times and measurements. |

A gap, an incomplete or still-open interval, a nonqualifying interval, or a switch in the
containers' roles cannot join two observations into a finding. A missing member row is not
a zero. An independent, fully evidenced pair of intervals is still reportable.

## Evidence and limits

Example: from 09:00–09:05 UTC, `Import` and `Checkout` each receive 500 requests.
`Import` consumes 4,500 RU with no throttles; `Checkout` consumes 500 RU and has 50
throttles. `Import` used 90% of the measured RU, `Checkout` throttled at 10%, and the pool
throttled at 5%. This is one observation. If the same pair does this again from 09:05–09:10,
it is High. If instead `Import` ran in the morning and `Checkout` throttled in the afternoon,
identical daily totals do **not** make a contention finding. A victim consuming 21% of the
active members' RU fails the victim-share gate.
Dominance alone, balanced throttling or a single active collection is not this finding.
No telemetry is insufficient data; failed queries are explicit query errors. A positive
pool throttle rate with no measured RU cannot establish a consumption split.

This is an inference from returned database traffic, not a measurement of the service's
internal RU allocation. Verify collection throughput ownership and workload isolation before
attributing contention. The active-collection filter also means the proportions are not a
complete bill/account inventory. Source failures are not grounds for silently trying another table.

## Remediation and references

Load
[`throughput-container-vs-database`](https://github.com/AzureCosmosDB/cosmosdb-agent-kit/blob/main/skills/cosmosdb-best-practices/rules/throughput-container-vs-database.md).
Investigate the named workloads during the reported times. For a one-time import, try pacing
or rescheduling it before changing throughput. **Only if the workloads must run together and
need isolation** should dedicated throughput be evaluated. Logs alone do not justify telling
the customer to migrate. CODA neither reallocates capacity nor moves collections.
