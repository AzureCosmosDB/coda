# DX-003: Shared-throughput starvation

[Catalog](../detectors.md) | [Log prerequisites](../detections/log-analytics-readiness.md)

## Data and scope

A database-level check selected for shared-throughput offers. It compares per-collection
request counts, 429 counts and consumed RU in the database over one day. It prefers
`CDBDataPlaneRequests5M`, with raw `CDBDataPlaneRequests` compatibility when the aggregate
source is unavailable. Aggregate counts sum represented requests, not rows.
Five-minute windows end at a completed bucket; explicit windows must align with that source.

## Detection and severity

| Configuration | Default |
|---|---|
| `min_requests` | `100` |
| `pool_throttle_pct` | `5.0` |
| `dominance_pct` | `60.0` |
| `victim_throttle_pct` | `5.0` |
| `victim_share_pct` | `20.0` |

Only collections with at least 100 requests participate in the classified totals.
At least two must be active. Their combined 429 rate must be at least 5%, the largest
RU consumer must hold at least 60% of their RU, and a different collection must throttle
at least 5% while consuming at most 20%. All boundary comparisons are inclusive.
A matching pattern is **High** severity.

## Evidence and limits

For two equally busy collections with 80%/20% RU share, a pool 429 rate of 5% and
victim 429 rate of 5% meet the gates. A victim consuming 21% fails the victim-share gate.
Dominance alone, balanced throttling or a single active collection is not this finding.
No telemetry is insufficient data; failed queries are explicit query errors. A positive
pool throttle rate with no measured RU cannot establish a consumption split.

This is an inference from returned database traffic, not a measurement of the service's
internal RU allocation. Verify collection throughput ownership and workload isolation before
attributing contention. The active-collection filter also means the proportions are not a
complete bill/account inventory. Source failures are not grounds for silently trying another table.
