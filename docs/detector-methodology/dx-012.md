# DX-012: Manual-to-autoscale candidate

[Catalog](../detectors.md) | [Access and sign-in](../permissions.md)

## Data and scope

For a dedicated manual offer, show the measured `NormalizedRUConsumption` hourly maxima
relative to its configured RU/s. These are peaks, not the average consumption within each
hour. A new container is eligible for observations; do not use account age as measured history.

## Detection and severity

| Configuration | Default |
|---|---|
| `measured_review_hours` | `168` |
| `measured_advice_hours` | `720` |

Use the latest consecutive completed UTC hours. With fewer than 168, show measurements only.
With a complete week but not a complete month, a favorable comparison prompts an ungraded
review; there is no suggested setting or switch. With a complete month, a strictly cheaper
autoscale estimate is a **Medium**, proposal-threshold finding. No recurrence, peak-to-average
ratio, or burst-count requirement applies. An older complete week cannot fill a recent gap.
Leading zeros that could predate the resource do not count without independent existence
evidence or prior positive utilization on that offer.

Compare manual throughput with an **equal autoscale maximum**. In each hour manual pays for
the configured capacity; model autoscale at the larger of that hour's peak percentage or 10%
of the same capacity. Apply the floor **before** summing. The two modes share a throughput
meter within each region of a paid provisioned account. Single-write autoscale uses 1.5 times
the quantity; manual uses 1.25 in an explicitly zone-redundant region, otherwise 1.
Multi-write uses its own meter at the **same per-unit rate for both modes**, with neither premium.
Each region's unit price cancels locally, not across regions.

## Evidence and limits

For a manual 10,000 RU/s offer at 50% every hour, in one non-zone-redundant region,
manual uses 100 meter units per hour; autoscale models 50 × 1.5 = 75. After one measured week,
review whether it is representative. After a measured month, consider autoscale **at 10,000
RU/s**, not a smaller maximum. At 80%, autoscale models 120 units, so no switch is advised.
A single burst is counted in its own hour. Missing readings are not zero demand.

For confirmed traditional autoscale, the **hottest partition or region** scales the whole offer:
use the offer's hourly peak in each region's projection. For dynamic autoscale, measure the
hourly normalized peak **separately in every region**; using its busiest partition is a
conservative upper estimate for that region. A missing region cannot be replaced with another.
For example, traditional single-write utilization peaking at 90% in West and 10% in East
models 90% in both, not the 50% average. At a verified multi-write site, 80% in both regions
can favor autoscale because there is no 1.5 multiplier.

Advise only if the alternate mode is no more expensive in every region and strictly cheaper
in at least one. Opposing regional estimates need **region-specific unit prices**; CODA does
not use an account-wide blended price to settle them. Unknown scaling mode in a multi-region
account, missing required zone settings, free tier, or an unsupported equal-capacity setting
also withhold advice. The score does not include reservations, negotiated commitments, future
growth, or service-specific conversion minima. The fixed monthly setting is an assumption:
confirm the configuration and that the measured period represents the expected workload.
If partitions scale independently, projecting the busiest partition's normalized peak onto
the whole offer can overestimate autoscale cost; a negative comparison is not proof of no savings.
